# How Homework Is Graded

Every homework assignment in this course is graded the same way, with the same
three criteria and the same nine points. Learn it once and it applies all term.

## The three criteria

Each assignment is scored on three criteria. Each criterion is worth 0 to 3
points, for **9 points total**.

### Submission — did the work arrive in a form I can grade?

| | |
|---|---|
| **3** | Everything is where the handout said to put it, with the names the handout used, and it was pushed with `save`. |
| **2** | Something small was off — a misnamed file, work in the wrong folder — but I could still find and grade it. |
| **1** | Something got in the way of grading: I had to hunt for your work, or it was not pushed. |
| **0** | Nothing was submitted. |

This criterion is not about how good the work is. It is about whether it
arrived in the shape the handout asked for. Following the directions here costs
you almost nothing and makes grading fast enough that you get your work back
quickly — which is the whole point of the turnaround schedule in the syllabus.

**Your work is not submitted until you run `save`.** Editing files in
Codespaces is not submitting. If you are unsure, run `save` again; it is safe
to run as many times as you like.

### Completeness — was every problem attempted?

| | |
|---|---|
| **3** | Everything was attempted. A small oversight is fine. |
| **2** | Something significant was skipped. |
| **1** | Multiple significant items were skipped. |
| **0** | Nothing was submitted. |

This is a quick check for *attempted*, not for *correct*. A serious attempt
that does not work counts as attempted. A blank file does not.

If you get stuck, submit what you have with a comment describing where you got
stuck and what you tried. That is worth real points here, and it tells me
something I can actually help with.

### Correctness — does the work show you understand the core concepts?

| | |
|---|---|
| **3** | Yes. |
| **2** | A core concept is misunderstood. |
| **1** | Multiple key understandings are missing. |
| **0** | Little or no understanding of the core concepts is evident. |

This is about **concepts, not details**. Every assignment handout names the core
concepts it is built around, in its Purpose section. Those are what this
criterion looks at.

A typo, an awkward regex, a rule you could have written more simply — none of
those cost you points here. A misunderstanding of what a `skip` rule does,
does.

## What the scores come to

| Score | Percent | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 9 / 9 | 100% | A |
| 8 / 9 | 88.9% | B+ |
| 7 / 9 | 77.8% | C+ |
| 6 / 9 | 66.7% | D+ |
| 5 / 9 | 55.6% | F |

Assignment scores average together into the homework portion of your grade,
which is 25% of the course. The letters above are the equivalent on the
syllabus grading scale, to give you a feel for what a score means.

Two things worth reading off that table.

**Attempting everything and submitting it correctly earns 6 of 9 — a D+.** That
is deliberate. Doing the work and turning it in properly is the baseline
expectation, not an achievement.

**Understanding is what moves you from a D+ to an A.** The entire meaningful
range of the grade lives in the Correctness criterion. That is also deliberate:
what I care about is whether you understand the material, and that is what the
grade should reflect.

## Why it is built this way

Because it separates **feedback** from **points**.

When a rubric assigns points to every small thing, every comment an instructor
writes becomes a justification for a deduction, and students read comments
defensively — or skip them and read only the number.

Here, notes on details cost you nothing. If I point out that a regex could be
tighter, or suggest a clearer token name, or flag a habit that will bite you in
three weeks, that is advice. It is not a deduction and there is nothing to
appeal. **Read the comments.** They are the part of grading that is actually
worth your time, and on this rubric they are free for me to give and free for
you to receive.

It also means a comment saying "this concept is not solid yet" is real
information about what to study, rather than an accounting note.

## Late work, and the last assignment

Two situations change how the rubric is applied. They sound alike and are
opposites, so here are all three cases together.

| | Submission | Completeness | Correctness | Best possible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **On time**, including the grace period | scored | scored | scored | 9 / 9 |
| **Late** — past grace, up to one week | 2 at best | scored | **0** | **5 / 9** |
| **The last assignment of the term** | scored | scored | **full credit** | 9 / 9 |

### Late work

Submitting inside the **72-hour grace period is not late**. Nothing changes and
it costs you nothing. That is what the grace period is for, and you do not need
to ask for it.

Past the grace period and up to one week late, two things happen. Submission
drops to **2 at best** — being late is itself an impediment to grading, and
anything else wrong with the submission can lower it further. And **Correctness
earns 0**, not because your work is wrong but because I am not going to check
it. That leaves Completeness as the only criterion still in play, and a ceiling
of **5 out of 9** — the half credit the syllabus describes.

After one week, work is not accepted and receives a 0.

### The last assignment of the term

The final assignment is graded for completeness *by design*, and it works the
opposite way: **Correctness is given full credit**, not zero. Nothing is being
withheld from you. In place of individual feedback, solutions are released
before the review session for the final exam, so you can check your own work
while you study.

## When you get work back

You will get a score on each of the three criteria and comments on your work.
Look at which criterion cost you points, because they mean different things:

- Lost points on **Submission** — a process habit to fix. Cheap to fix, and it
  will keep costing you until you do.
- Lost points on **Completeness** — you ran out of time or skipped something.
  Start earlier; submit partial work with notes.
- Lost points on **Correctness** — a concept has not landed. This is the one to
  bring to office hours or Discord, and the one worth studying before the next
  exam.

If you think a score is wrong, ask. If you want to understand a comment, ask
that too — that conversation is more useful than the score.
